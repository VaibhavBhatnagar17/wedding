**Dates:** 31 Jan (arrivals) · 1 Feb (Haldi, Ring Ceremony + Sangeet) · 2 Feb 2027 (Phere, Reception)
**Phere muhurat:** 2 Feb 2027, 11:00 – 13:30
**City:** Udaipur, Rajasthan
**Budget:** ₹20,00,000 all-in
**Guests:** ~300 (family, both sides) for Haldi / Ring + Sangeet / Phere · ~650 for Reception

---

## 1. The three decisions that make ₹20L work

Before anything else, be clear about what this budget can and cannot buy in peak-season Udaipur.

**1,445 meal covers is the binding constraint.** 300 guests × 3 functions + 650 at the reception is ~1,445 plates. Even at a lean ₹700/plate that is ₹10L — half the budget — before a single flower is bought. Every other decision follows from this.

Three structural choices, in order of impact:

**(a) The venue must be a garden / mid-tier resort, not a palace.**
A luxury property (Taj Lake Palace, Oberoi Udaivilas, Leela, Raffles) starts at ₹1Cr+. A premium resort (Trident, Ananta, Westin, Fateh Garh) runs ₹35–70L for a wedding of this size. Both are out. Your tier is a **heritage or mid-resort with a 700+ capacity lawn, or a marriage garden that permits your own caterer** — where ₹650–800/plate is genuinely achievable at volume. This is not a compromise on beauty; Udaipur's mid-tier properties have Aravalli backdrops and lake views that photograph better than most five-stars elsewhere. It *is* a compromise on brand name and room luxury.

**(b) Guest rooms are blocked, not bought.**
100+ rooms × 2 nights would be ₹14L+ on its own. Standard destination-wedding practice: you negotiate a block rate and guests pay their own rooms; the host covers only immediate family and VIPs. **This plan budgets 10 host-paid rooms** (₹60,000), assuming you extract 8–10 complimentary rooms against your ~₹10L F&B commitment. If you intend to pay for all guest rooms, the guest count has to come down to roughly 120 / 300 — tell me and I'll rebuild the plan.

**(c) Engagement merges into the Sangeet evening.**
A standalone engagement means another dinner for 285, another décor setup and another venue slot — about **₹2.6L**. Instead: a 30-minute ring ceremony at 19:00 on the sangeet stage, before the performances. It reads as intentional ("Ring Ceremony & Sangeet Night"), it's better paced for guests, and it is the single biggest saving available to you.

**Assumed excluded from the ₹20L** — confirm these, because they change everything:
heirloom / investment jewellery · shagun and cash gifts · guests' airfare and train fare · guests' room tariffs · honeymoon.

Bride's and groom's outfits, hair and makeup, and all five functions **are** inside the ₹20L.

## 2. Why this date is in your favour

1 Feb 2027 is a **Monday**, 2 Feb a **Tuesday**. Weekday weddings in Udaipur price 10–15% below Saturday–Sunday, and the good vendors are available. On a budget this tight, a weekday date is worth roughly ₹1.2L — do not move it to a weekend.

February is peak season, so book early anyway. Weather: days 25–27°C, nights 10–12°C, sunrise ~07:05, **sunset ~18:10**. Two consequences built into the schedule below: outdoor daytime functions must wrap by 17:30, and every evening function needs patio heaters and a shawl favour.

**Verify with your pandit and a 2027 panchang:** whether 2 Feb sits in a shubh vivah muhurat window, and whether Basant Panchami (approximately 11 Feb 2027) or another abooj muhurat falls close enough to cluster vendor bookings. If 1–2 Feb turns out to be a heavy muhurat date, expect +15% on rates and book 14+ months out.

## 6. Décor — ₹2,00,000 across four setups

The strategy is one palette per day, structures reused, and spend concentrated on **ceiling, stage and entry** — the only three things a camera sees.

**Haldi — ₹35,000 · "Marigold Morning"**
Marigold and genda torans, yellow-orange gota chandeliers, bamboo and cane props, earthen pots, chatris, umbrella ceiling, a tyre swing, dhol corner, hand-painted "Haldi" board. Daylight means zero lighting spend — that's why it's a morning function.

**Ring Ceremony + Sangeet — ₹45,000**
This is a lighting function, not a flower function. Mirror-mosaic stage backdrop, fairy-light canopy over the dance floor, 12 uplighters, 2 moving heads, a 12×8 ft LED wall (~₹18k, also your video screen and monogram display), fuchsia-emerald-gold drapes, low seating with bolsters and mirrored tables, hanging umbrellas. The ring ceremony uses the same stage plus one 4×4 ft floral arch rolled on and off.

**Baraat + Varmala + Mandap — ₹65,000 · the hero setup**
Four-pillar open mandap, ivory and soft pink, hanging jasmine and rajnigandha strings, kalash and banana leaves, fabric canopy, petal-path aisle with brass urlis, 300 chairs with white covers and coloured sashes, a toran gate for the baraat, and a raised varmala stage with a flower-shower machine.

**Reception — ₹43,000**
Dramatic backdrop with the couple's monogram (~₹20k), 20 uplighters, entrance arch, 6 photo-wall panels, candles and tall centrepieces on 30 cocktail tables. After dark, lighting does all the work.

**The décor playbook**
- Marigold runs ₹40–60/kg in February; orchids are ₹90 a stem. Go 80% marigold, rose, carnation, rajnigandha and jasmine. Zero imported flowers.
- Use the venue's in-house or preferred decorator — 20–25% cheaper, no outside-vendor entry fee, and they already know the property's rigging points.
- Rent, never build: LED wall, moving heads, low seating, cold pyro.
- Reuse across functions: the haldi's cane and umbrella props become the reception photo corner; the sangeet's fairy lights become the reception ceiling.
- Ask for a **photograph of a past setup at your exact venue** before signing, not a Pinterest mood board.

## 7. Menus — designed to hit the per-plate targets

**Haldi brunch · ₹475**
Kesar lassi, jaljeera, nimbu shikanji · chaat counter (raj kachori, dahi bhalla, papdi) · live aloo tikki and chole bhature · poha, upma, medu vada · 2 mains + dal + rice + live paratha counter · live jalebi-rabri, moong dal halwa · sweet paan.

**Ring + Sangeet dinner · ₹700**
6 veg starters, 2 of them live (hara bhara kebab, paneer tikka, mushroom galouti, corn cheese balls, honey chilli potato, dahi ke sholay) · soup · 4 salads · Punjabi mains (paneer lababdar, dal makhani) · Rajasthani gatte ki sabzi · **one** live international counter — Chinese *or* Italian, not both (saves ₹60/plate) · tandoori roti and naan counter · biryani · gulab jamun, baked rasmalai, live kulfi falooda, ice cream cart.

**Wedding lunch · ₹550 · Rajasthani thali**
Dal Baati Churma, Gatte ki Sabzi, Ker Sangri, Papad ki Sabzi, Bajra and missi roti, Bajre ki Khichdi, kadhi, rice, salads, chutneys · Malpua and Ghewar · buttermilk and jaljeera. Seated service with limited counters — cheaper than a buffet, more authentic, and exactly what guests want after 2.5 hours at a mandap.

**Reception dinner · ₹700 at 600 covers**
6 starters (2 live) · soup and salad bar · North Indian mains ×4 · one Rajasthani signature · live pasta *or* noodle counter · dal, rice, biryani, live Indian bread counter · chaat corner · 5 desserts including one live · paan counter.

**Cost rules for the caterer negotiation:** all-veg (non-veg at Indian weddings raises per-plate 25–35% and complicates the kitchen), no seafood, no imported ingredients, no more than one live international counter per function, and desserts weighted to Indian sweets rather than plated patisserie.

**Dietary tracking matters here.** Jain and no-onion-no-garlic guests need a separate labelled counter — the website tracks this per guest and gives you a count to hand the caterer.

**Bar.** ₹70,000 buys a 2-hour open bar for ~105 drinkers: 2 whisky, 1 vodka, 1 gin, 1 rum, beer and 2 wines, all IMFL, no imported labels. Rajasthan requires an **occasional (temporary) bar licence** for serving liquor at a private function — the venue or a licensed vendor must apply about two weeks ahead. Budget alternatives: a cash bar (₹0 to you) or premium mocktails only.

## 8. Risks, and what to do about them

**Outdoor loudspeakers must stop at 22:00.** Rajasthan Pollution Control Board rules, following the Supreme Court order, prohibit open-air amplified sound between 22:00 and 06:00. Udaipur venues enforce this. The schedule already moves the after-party indoors at 22:00 — get the venue's indoor-music policy **in writing**.

**Night temperatures of 10–12°C.** Budget 12 patio heaters at ₹800–1,200/day (~₹15k for two nights, from contingency) and give shawls or stoles as reception favours. Guests will remember being cold far longer than they'll remember the centrepieces.

**Sunset at 18:10.** All outdoor daytime functions finish by 17:30. Test the reception lighting at 17:00, not at 19:00.

**Drone permissions.** Udaipur has restricted airspace near Dabok airport and over the lakes. Confirm your cinematographer flies a DGCA-compliant, registered drone and get written venue clearance.

**Indoor backup.** February rain is unlikely but not impossible. Get written confirmation that the venue can move 300 (sangeet) and 650 (reception) indoors.

**Old-city traffic.** Peak season around Lake Pichola and the City Palace is genuinely bad. Keep the venue out of the old city or add a 45-minute buffer to every transfer.

**Payments.** Never pay more than 30% as advance. Hold 30% until after the event. Insist on a written cancellation and postponement clause in every contract — including the venue's.

**Catering guarantees.** Venues bill on max(guarantee, actual). Commit **275** for the family functions and **600** for the reception, never the full invite count, and negotiate a 5% flex clause with final numbers due 72 hours out. Expect 88–92% attendance from invited family at a destination wedding and 85–90% at the reception.
